        <h1>I have ideas</h1>
        <p>I've had a note on my phone that has a bunch of business ideas and improvements for things that bug me.</p>
        <p>Improved slippers. DIY drink kits. Compostable packaging. Dog products. Picnic gear. Yoga studios. Payment apps. Sporting goods. Freeze-dried coffee. Smart towels. Electric jerry cans. Marketplaces. And cheese made from donkey's milk, idk..</p>
        <p>Some of the  ideas are terrible. Some are probably impossible, for me. A few might actually be ok. The annoying part is that none of them have ever become anything.</p>
        <p>For a long time I thought the problem was finding the right idea. I would always think that this is not quite the right idea and if I just had the right one, everything would fall into place. Looking back at this list of things, I had the opposite problem. I was good at collecting ideas and noticing problems.</p>
        <p>Every time I saw something interesting I'd think, "That could be something." I'd write it down, maybe add a few notes about why it was something, then forget it and move on to the next thing. The note kept growing. My commitment to one single idea didnt. It felt productive because I was thinking? But I’m not sure thinking has got me anywhere.</p>
        <p>I never did anything else. I never did further research. I never called suppliers. I never built a website. I never tried to sell one product. I never found out whether anyone actually cared.</p>
        <p>My notebook became an outdated list of idea that only made it as far as my notes app, with some problems that have sat there so long someone else has solved them.</p>
        <p>I don’t think my ideas are bad, but I do think it’s bad that I didn’t start learning from just running with them.</p>
        <p>My old notes remind me of a version of myself who believed success came from finding or waiting for the perfect opportunity to come along. But now I think success comes from picking an imperfect one and seeing it through and becoming really good at doing that one thing.</p>
